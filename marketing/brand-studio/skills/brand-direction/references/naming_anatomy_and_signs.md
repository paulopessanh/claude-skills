# Naming, anatomy and signs

Confidence key: 🟢 published primary source · 🟡 secondary, or confirm locally · 🔴 folklore.

## Anatomy of a brand (the working standard)

A brand is a set of concepts, forms, colours and letters composed in harmony.

| Type | Parts |
|---|---|
| Nominative | the name alone |
| Figurative | a symbol alone (emblem or icon) |
| Mixed | symbol + name (default) |
| Three-dimensional | form or object as the mark |

Baseline: **descriptor** (required, says what the company does), **tagline** (required, carries essence and positioning),
**slogan** (optional, tied to a campaign or product), **hookline** (optional, a repeatable phrase for buzz; needs a marketing
strategy behind it). `anatomy_validator.py` enforces the required ones and forces an explicit decision on the optional ones.

## A name reading

Names often arrive closed (from the client or a third party). Read them for: origin, pronunciation, spelling, memorability,
meaning, connotations in each market and language, the abbreviations the market will use, and what the name asks of the drawing.
The script handles the mechanical part (length, syllables, ambiguous letters, closeness to competitors, vetoed terms,
age of registry checks). A person handles the rest: read it aloud with three people from the audience, ask how they spell it,
check each market's language, and consult the trademark office.

## Signs: icon, index, symbol

Peirce's triad is a useful way to decide what a mark does: an **icon** resembles what it stands for, an **index** points to
it by a real connection, a **symbol** works by convention. A semantic formula states the mix on purpose ("an index of the
ridge, a symbol of patience"). A mark that is only symbol needs the audience to learn it; one that is only icon is easily copied.

## Tests for a form

For every shape: was it made, not copied from reality? Does it carry a pétreo? Does it work small, in one colour and in motion?
Test at 16, 32 and 128 pixels in black and white before any colour or mockup.

## Claims to hold loosely

- 🔴 "A name must describe the product." Many strong names are arbitrary or suggestive; descriptive names are the hardest to protect.
- 🔴 "Shorter is always better." The pull toward short names is real but the evidence for a fixed rule is thin; test with the audience.
- 🟡 Counts in the script (14 letters, 4 syllables) are heuristics for a warning, not limits.
