# Review layers and the client's lens

Confidence key: 🟢 published primary source · 🟡 secondary or judgment · 🔴 folklore.

## Why a layered, independent review

Reviewers miss different things. Several independent readings find more problems than one, and a reader who has seen the
author's reasoning tends to agree with it. So: run the deterministic lint first, then read with a fresh context, then compare
two independent readings on the steps that carry the most weight (direction, brand and identity, manual).

The nine layers and what each catches:

| Layer | Catches |
|---|---|
| Facts | names, numbers and dates that differ across documents; claims with no source; quotes nobody said |
| Temporal | "16 years of client" in a company that is 7 years old |
| Process | a vetado used as a detail; a change not applied everywhere; an approved decision contradicted without a record |
| Concept and anatomy | missing descriptor or tagline; formula not kept |
| Visual | contrast not computed; HEX, RGB and CMYK that disagree; unlicensed fonts |
| Narrative | no moment of revelation; no decision asked |
| Text | house-style violations, jargon, hedges |
| Technical | file names, slide geometry, links, delivery folder |
| Schedule and scope | work outside the contract; a date that cannot be met |

## The client's eyes

Two lenses. The decisor who approves, and the client's own customer, because the brand has to work for whoever buys from the
client and not only for whoever pays the studio. For each decisor: what they understand in 30 seconds, what they will ask,
what bothers them, what they will want to show someone. Then an adherence map from each briefing objective to where the
deliverable answers it. Then a list of studio biases (aesthetics over business, jargon, references only designers know, taste
ignored without explanation, a vetoed colour hidden in a detail, a strong conclusion on thin evidence, a lecturing tone).

## Premortem

Before a major presentation, imagine it failed and write why. Teams that do this surface risks that optimism hides.

## Handling a change request

Record the literal words. Find what problem they solve (a request is usually a solution to a fear). Triage against locked
decisions and the step graph. Classify accept, negotiate or defend, and write a short answer. Defending the client's goal can mean
disagreeing with the client's request; do it with an alternative that respects the system.

## Claims to hold loosely

- 🔴 "The client is always right" and 🔴 "the designer knows best". Both end the conversation. The job is to find what the client needs and defend it.
- 🟡 Rejection-risk ratings (high, medium, low) are judgment, not measurement. Always give the reason next to the rating.
